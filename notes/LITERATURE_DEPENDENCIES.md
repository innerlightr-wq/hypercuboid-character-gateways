## 1. Shioda–Tate formula

**Citation**: T. Shioda, "On elliptic modular surfaces," *J. Math. Soc.
Japan* **24** (1972), 20–59.

**Theorem used**: for a relatively minimal elliptic surface
`\pi:X\to C` over a field `L` with section, with trivial lattice
`\mathrm{Triv}(X)=U\oplus\bigoplus_v(\text{root lattice of the fiber at }v)`,
$$\rho(X_L)=\mathrm{rank}\,\mathrm{Triv}(X)+\mathrm{rank}\,MW(X/L).$$
**Hypotheses**: relatively minimal elliptic surface with a section over
a field `L`. **Our surface satisfies this**: `X\to\mathbb P^1_t`,
minimal Weierstrass model, zero section `O`. **Imported conclusion**:
`\rho(X_{\overline{\mathbb Q}})=19+\mathrm{rank}\,MW(X/\overline{\mathbb Q}(t))`.

## 2. Shioda's height pairing / Mordell–Weil lattices

**Citation**: T. Shioda, "On the Mordell-Weil lattices," *Comment.
Math. Univ. St. Paul.* **39** (1990), no. 2, 211–240.

**Results used**: (a) the height formula
`\hat h(P)=2\chi(\mathcal O_X)+2(P\cdot O)-\sum_v\mathrm{contr}_v(P)`;
(b) the universal local-contribution table for `I_n` and `I_n^*`
fibers; (c) `\hat h(P)=0\iff P` torsion; (d) the discriminant formula
for the Mordell–Weil lattice of a rank-1 generator. **Hypotheses**:
same elliptic-surface setup as §1, `P` a section. **Our surface
satisfies this** directly. **Imported conclusions**: `\hat h(P_1)=1`;
`P_1` non-torsion; `|\mathrm{disc}\,NS(X)|=8`.

## 3. Singular K3 classification (Torelli + Shioda–Inose)

**Citations**: I. Pjatecki\u{i}-\u{S}apiro, I. Šafarevič, "Torelli's
theorem for algebraic surfaces of type K3," *Izv. Akad. Nauk SSSR Ser.
Mat.* **35** (1971), 530–572 (Torelli theorem for K3 surfaces); T.
Shioda, H. Inose, "On singular K3 surfaces," in *Complex Analysis and
Algebraic Geometry*, Iwanami Shoten (1977), 119–136 (existence/
Shioda–Inose structure, surjectivity of the discriminant map).

**Theorem used**: `X\mapsto T(X)` is a bijection between isomorphism
classes of singular K3 surfaces (`\rho=20`) and isomorphism classes of
positive-definite even rank-2 lattices. **Hypotheses**: `X` a complex K3
surface with `\rho(X_{\mathbb C})=20`. **Our surface satisfies this**
(Theorem Ledger §III). **Imported conclusion**: the rank-2,
discriminant-8, positive-definite even lattice found is *the*
transcendental lattice of *the* singular K3 with that invariant, unique
up to isomorphism — combined with class-number-one uniqueness (§6
below) this pins down `T(X)\cong\mathrm{diag}(2,4)`. The paper's
explicit Shioda–Inose diagram (`E\times E'\dashrightarrow\mathrm{Km}(E\times E')\dashleftarrow X`,
both maps degree 2) is background only, not needed for the main
theorem (confirmed in the Round-34 audit; see Theorem Ledger).

## 4. Livné's modularity theorem for singular K3 surfaces

**Citation**: R. Livné, "Motivic orthogonal two-dimensional
representations of `\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/\mathbb Q)`,"
*Israel J. Math.* **92** (1995), 149–156. Precise statement and
applicability verified directly against the primary literature via M.
Schütt, "K3 surfaces with Picard rank 20" (arXiv:0804.1558), Theorem 4
and surrounding discussion — fetched and read during the Round-34 audit,
not relied on from memory.

**Theorem used** (Schütt's Theorem 4, attributed to Livné): every
singular K3 surface `X/\mathbb Q` is modular; the `L`-series of `T(X)`
is the Mellin transform of a Hecke eigenform of weight 3 with CM by
`K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)`, `d=\mathrm{disc}\,T(X)<0`. **Hypotheses**: `X`
a singular K3 surface defined over `\mathbb Q`. **Verified applicable
regardless of whether `NS(X)` is generated by `\mathbb Q$-rational
classes** (explicitly confirmed in the source — covers our
`\rho(X/\mathbb Q)=19` situation). **Imported conclusion**: `L`-series
equality forces exact Frobenius-trace equality of `T(X)` and (a
quadratic twist of) the newform at every good prime — used in Theorem
Ledger Part IV.

## 5. Twist-classification of rational CM newforms

**Citation**: M. Schütt, "K3 surfaces with Picard rank 20" (arXiv:0804.1558),
Theorem 6, itself building on: M. Schütt, "CM newforms with rational
coefficients," *Ramanujan J.* (2008/09) (arXiv:math/0511228).

**Theorem used**: for `K` imaginary quadratic with class group of
exponent `\le2`, the weight-3, CM-by-`K` newforms with rational
Fourier coefficients are exactly the quadratic twists of a single
canonical eigenform. **Hypotheses**: `\mathrm{Cl}(K)\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^g`.
**Our field `K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2})` satisfies this** (`h(-8)=1`, trivial
class group, vacuously exponent `\le2`). **Imported conclusion**:
exactly one such newform up to twist — used to justify the finite,
theorem-forced candidate set `\{1,\chi(-1),\chi(2),\chi(-2)\}` in
Theorem Ledger Part IV, and the LMFDB identification of `8.3.d.a` as
the specific tabulated representative.

## 6. Class number of `\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2})`

**Citation**: elementary; not a deep external theorem, but stated for
completeness. Standard consequence of the Minkowski bound
(`M_K=\frac2\pi\sqrt{|d_K|}=\frac2\pi\sqrt8\approx1.80<2`, so every
ideal class contains an ideal of norm `\le1`, forcing `h(-8)=1`); see
any standard algebraic number theory text (e.g. Neukirch, *Algebraic
Number Theory*, Ch. I §6, or Stark's tables of imaginary quadratic
fields of class number 1).

**Imported conclusion**: `h(-8)=1` — used twice: (a) uniqueness of the
discriminant-8 lattice (§3); (b) uniqueness (up to twist) of the
CM newform (§5).

## 7. CM symmetric-square decomposition

**Citation**: K. Ribet, "Galois representations attached to eigenforms
with nebentypus," in *Modular Functions of One Variable V*, Lecture
Notes in Math. **601**, Springer (1977), 17–52. (General fact that
`\mathrm{Sym}^2` of a CM elliptic curve's Galois representation splits
as a CM-twisted cyclotomic character plus a weight-3 CM-newform
representation.)

**Hypotheses**: `E` an elliptic curve with CM by an imaginary quadratic
order. **Our `E:y^2=x^3+4x^2+2x` satisfies this** (CM by
`\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-2}]`). **Imported conclusion**: the *existence and
shape* of the splitting
`\mathrm{Sym}^2(\rho_E)\cong\rho_g\oplus(\chi_K\cdot\mathrm{cyc})` for
some weight-3 CM-by-`K` form `g` — the actual trace identity
`a_p(f)=a_p(E)^2-p(1+\chi(-2))` follows from this plus elementary
Frobenius-eigenvalue algebra (self-contained, Theorem Ledger Part IX),
and `g=f` by §5's uniqueness.

## 8. LMFDB (identification only, not a theorem)

**Used for**: retrieving the tabulated Fourier coefficients (`a_p(f)`)
of the newform of weight 3, level 8, CM by `\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2})`,
labeled `8.3.d.a`, via the raw API (`mf_newforms` `traces` field), not
an AI-summarized fetch. **Not used as, or cited as, the proof of any
theorem** — its role is purely to name and tabulate the specific
eigenform whose existence and essential uniqueness (up to twist) is
established by §5, and to provide the one deductively-used numerical
value `a_3(f)=-2` (Theorem Ledger Part IV / Round 34 audit).
